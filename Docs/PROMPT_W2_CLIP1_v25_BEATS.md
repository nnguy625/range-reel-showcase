## THE TWO DEFECTS THIS VERSION REMOVED — both Pablo's, both from the same failure class

**A-14 — the mass-unison "signature frame."** I wrote the whole street landing one shape "gone
immediately, on the very next beat." *The landing took; the removal did not.* It became a ~3-second
frozen arms-out tableau across ~10 bodies. **A stillness instruction beats a motion instruction every
time.** Franco's law applies to the ENSEMBLE, not just the hero: **path → shape appears at accent →
next impulse removes it.** Any unison beat must name the removing impulse for every body it names.

**A-13 — the coordination prohibition.** I wrote *"her feet carry the beat while her arms carry the
melody, and the two are never doing the same thing at the same time."* That clause forbids exactly
what Nelson asked for ("more coordinated arm & leg movements") AND let the model satisfy "different
things" by leaving the legs inert while the arms danced. Replaced with: **step and arm shape arrive on
the same accent, so the whole figure reads as one silhouette.**

**Third fix:** every beat gives the ensemble a *different* job. Identical ensemble instruction across
beats renders as a frozen crowd.

## THE PROMPT

One continuous 8-second take, real time, no internal cuts, no transitions, no dissolves. Normal speed
throughout — no slow motion, no ramping, no speed change. Eight seconds in which her feet and her arms
stay continuously entangled, without a single second repeating what came before.

A woman in a crimson saree lands barefoot in a crowded bazaar and dances in the middle of the street
while everyone around her dances too.

@Image 1 is the exact first frame. Begin on @Image 1 exactly, with no reframing and no establishing
shot before it. The woman in crimson is Paola. Every dancer who appears in the shot is already standing
in frame one and already moving, and a few onlookers are already leaning on the balcony railings above.
Only one woman in crimson is ever in frame, and no new person walks into the shot at any point.

@Pao-Face-Bolly governs Paola's face and nothing else — it has no authority over her body, her costume,
the other dancers, or the location.

GEO SPATIAL LAYOUT (locked across every W2 shot — a spatial map only):

— Spice sacks and brass tables sit along the left side of frame; textile stalls along the right side.
— The stone centre of the street is open ground with no stalls on it — this is where the dancing happens.
— Wooden balconies overhang both sides above the stalls.
— The sun sits low and centred in the far opening, behind the dancers and facing camera, so every shadow
  on the stone runs toward camera.
— The camera stays on the near side of the corridor for the whole shot and never travels around to the
  far side.

FRAME
At frame one the perspective, lens geometry, camera height and subject scale match @Image 1 exactly.
From there the focal length never changes and the field of view never drifts — the only thing that
changes is how far the camera is from her. The camera sits below her eye line and looks slightly up.
Paola stays large enough in frame that her feet and her hands are both clearly readable for the whole

with clean headroom. Depth stays layered: cloth, dust and passing dancers move close to camera in the
foreground; Paola and the nearest dancers sharp in the middle distance; the receding rows, the balconies
and the sun behind her. Focus holds on Paola; close foreground bodies and far rows fall soft.

CAMERA THROUGH-LINE
One unbroken handheld pullback for the entire shot, carrying small operator drift and micro-correction.
It changes speed but never comes to a hard stop and never jumps back to a previous position. The retreat
is restrained and never lets her get small. Per-beat camera behaviour is specified in the beats below.

ACTION — SIX BEATS, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE
The attached track is the only timing clock; the pulse is 128 beats per minute. Paola is in visible
motion in every single frame. Her arms and legs are one body: on every accent her step and her arm shape
arrive at the same instant, so her whole figure lands the shape together and reads as one clear
silhouette. Every hand shape appears as her hand travels through it and the next foot impulse
immediately pulls it onward — no hand parks, no body holds, nothing waits. The rhythm underneath
everything is SINGLE, SINGLE, DOUBLE, DOUBLE: each single one springing step, each double two fast
rebounds on the same foot, mirrored right then left. The rhythm repeats; the movement inside it never does.

0.0–0.8s · THE DROP BECOMES THE FIRST STEP
Action: Both bare feet strike the stone together, wide apart, on the first heavy bass note. Ankles and
knees fold to absorb it and the floor throws her straight back up onto the balls of her feet. Her high
arm is already falling diagonally across her torso as she rises, and before her heels touch again her
right foot has pushed off into the first travelling step. The dancers around her are already mid-phrase,
skirts swinging, none of them watching her land.
Camera: Retreating low and fast down the corridor, taking one hard jolt on the double-foot impact, still
moving as she rises.

0.8–2.3s · FIRST PASS OF THE HOOK, SMALL AND QUICK
Action: Her right foot springs diagonally forward-right while her left hand carves up from her right hip
across her torso to above her left temple — foot and hand landing the beat together, the forearm turning
until the palm faces out, four fingers straight and pressed, thumb bent in. The wrist rebounds out of it
at once. She mirrors it forward-left. Then two fast rebounds on her right foot carry her forward-right
while her high hand drops to her sternum and the other opens to the side; two more carry her forward-left
while her rising wrist rolls open, all five fingers separating, radiating outward and curving gently back,
the hand travelling on through that open fan without stopping. The near dancers take the same four counts
a fraction behind her, each at her own reach.
Camera: Easing out of the fast retreat into a slow drift, holding her at the same size in frame.

2.3–3.9s · SECOND PASS, LARGER AND TRAVELLING
Action: The same four counts again, bigger. Each springing step covers more ground and turns her a
quarter as it lands, so the camera sees her from three-quarter, then profile, then three-quarter again.
Each wrist snap becomes a whole arm thrown overhead, and on the singles the four pressed fingers arrive
with the ring finger folding down. Her hips swing further and her ribcage counters harder on every step.
Behind her the ring widens — near dancers step outward and cross behind her back, opening the street.
Camera: Still drifting back, slower, a dancer sweeping close across the lens as she passes.

3.9–4.7s · THE DRUMS CUT IT
Action: Her weight rolls onto her right foot and her right hip drives out twice fast while her chest pops
sharply against it and her opposite shoulder gives one compact hit; on each accent her left middle
fingertip meets her thumb, the index curling inward, the ring and little fingers rising apart — then the
next foot impulse opens them again. Her left hip answers twice the same way. Every dancer cuts the same
two accents with her and breaks out of them immediately, nobody holding the shape.
Camera: Slowing almost to a standstill so her size barely changes, taking a short hard jolt on each stamp.

4.7–6.4s · THE STRINGS RETURN AND SHE TRAVELS
Action: The hard shapes flow straight back into sweeping. Her right foot crosses forward toward
frame-left, torso turning a quarter with it, while her left arm sweeps from her right hip across her face
and overhead, forearm rotating so the palm keeps changing its angle to the light. Unwinding out of that
turn carries her left foot open toward frame-right into the opposite quarter-turn as her right arm mirrors
the path. Two fast side-steps travel her forward-right and two more forward-left, hips following the
stepping foot, ribcage redirecting the other way, wrists circling through all four. The far rows keep
travelling behind her at smaller scale.
Camera: Resuming the retreat, two near dancers crossing close in the foreground and breaking the sun into
brief flares.

6.4–8.0s · THE TURN AND OUT ON THE BUILD
Action: Her right foot steps diagonally toward camera and presses the stone while her left arm sweeps up
across her torso and pulls her shoulders into the rotation already loaded. Her left foot crosses through
and that rotation becomes one full travelling turn, torso rotating continuously over the supporting foot,
both arms continuing their circular overhead path without resetting, both hands passing through the open
fan as they rotate, her skirt flaring into a full circle. Two forward rebounds carry her out of the turn
toward camera without stopping, arms opening into a high-low asymmetry. Two final rebounds on the build
enlarge the same movement. Her arms are still opening, her skirt still travelling, her next foot already
off the stone and the whole street still moving when the clip cuts.
Camera: Retreating a last time as she comes out of the turn, still settling, not locked, when the clip cuts.

Paola starts every new movement first and performs the largest, clearest version of it — her steps travel
