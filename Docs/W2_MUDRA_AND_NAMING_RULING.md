## THE PREDICTION, COMMITTED BEFORE THE FIRE

## THE TEST DESIGN — HE KILLED THE PURE-NAMING ARM
> "**Do not test pure naming.** It answers a laboratory question but wastes the production fire."

**CONTROL** = v24 operations, unchanged.
**VARIANT** = the *same* operations with a move label prepended as a retrieval hint:
`A modernized Bollywood thumka, executed as follows: <unchanged operations>`
Only two labels, the two strong pointers: **modernized thumka** and **travelling chakkar**.
Explicitly NOT chaal/jhummar/jhatka — "three ambiguous style activations before SD2 reaches the
actual mechanics."

**Hip-hop stays operational, never a style label:**
> "'Thumka plus hip-hop' is broad. '**Thumka cut by an opposite chest pop and shoulder hit**' is
> executable." Use: knee bounce · chest pop · shoulder hit · sharp stop · rebound.

## FINGER DETAIL AT MEDIUM-WIDE — WHAT ACTUALLY SURVIVES
> "It pays as a generative steering signal, but not as a reliably auditable visual detail."

Survives: palm direction · open-vs-closed silhouette · finger-fan vs joined-blade (maybe).
Does not reliably survive: thumb-to-*specific*-fingertip contact — "motion blur and temporal
regeneration can collapse neighbouring fingers."

**Priority order for words:** arm pathway → forearm rotation → palm orientation → gross hand
silhouette → exact fingertip contact.
> "Spend one short anatomical clause per key endpoint, not forty words on every hand."

**Reduce to THREE visually distinct families** (Mayura and Hamsasya dropped — their identity depends
on fine fingertip relationships that will collapse into the same generic pinch at this size):
1. **joined blade** — Pataka / Tripataka
2. **pinched / ornamental** — Katakamukha / Bhramara
3. **fully open fan** — Alapadma

## 🔴 THE ANTI-POSING GRAMMAR — THE MOST VALUABLE THING IN THE RULING
Yes, exact hand endpoints CAN reintroduce posing. **Banned verbs: hold · settle · resolve.**

> **PATH → SHAPE APPEARS AT ACCENT → NEXT IMPULSE REMOVES IT.**
> **"The endpoint belongs to the beat, not to a pause."**

His model phrasings, adopted verbatim into the prompt:
- *Bhramara:* "The forearm rotates outward; on the accent the middle fingertip meets the thumb, the
  index curls inward and the ring and little fingers rise apart. The next foot rebound immediately
  pulls the wrist onward and opens the fingers into the following pathway."
- *Pataka:* "The forearm turns until the palm faces outward; four fingers straighten and press
  together while the thumb bends inward. The beat catches that flat silhouette, then the wrist
  immediately rebounds into the next circle."
- *Alapadma:* "The wrist rolls open as all five fingers separate, radiate outward and curve gently
  back; the hand continues traveling through the open fan without stopping."

## CULTURAL REGISTER
None of the seven hastas is wrong for Bollywood — it is a fusion form that routinely borrows from
classical, folk and Western vocabularies.
> "The risk is not cultural illegitimacy. The risk is **over-classical precision making the sequence
> feel like a hasta demonstration instead of film choreography.**"

Use freely: Pataka, Tripataka, Alapadma, Katakamukha.
Feature once: Bhramara, on the signature double. "Do not expect viewers to identify it by name."
Lowest return at this scale: Mayura, Hamsasya.
**Banked for future choreography:** *Ardhachandra* — four fingers together, thumb extended. "A strong
open profile that reads more clearly at distance than several fingertip-contact mudras."

## STILL OURS, NOT HIS — NELSON'S TWO GRAMMAR RULINGS
1. **The "while" grammar.** "she does X while doing Y & transitions to A while doing B." Binds the
   two halves of the fusion into ONE grammatical unit so they must co-occur; a listed pair invites
   averaging. Also encodes the visible polyrhythm (legs on one rhythm, arms on a slower one).
2. **Seamless sequencing.** Three connective laws now in the block:
   - weight always arrives on the foot the next step pushes from (verified: R→L→RR→LL chains
     correctly across all four phrase boundaries);
   - every mudra is a shape the hand passes THROUGH at the top of its path, never one it stops in;
   - recovery is preparation — each phrase's last count winds up the next phrase's first.
