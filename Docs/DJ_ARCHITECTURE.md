## WHY THE FIRST SIX OVERLAYS CAME BACK SLOW — MY ERROR

Tempo only defines the grid. **Perceived speed comes from event density and articulation, not BPM.**

| Makes 128 BPM feel SLOW | Makes 128 BPM feel FAST |
|---|---|
| long sustained notes, legato strings | constant eighth- and sixteenth-note motion |
| low-register drones, pads | short staccato attacks, plucks, chops |
| slow chord changes, broad melodies | ostinatos, fast call-and-response |
| empty space, half-time phrasing | upper-mid-register hooks, offbeat syncopation |
| long reverb | tremolo, faster harmonic changes, narrow reverb |
| | two interlocking melodic layers |

**I wrote the left column into every prompt.** "Long sustained phrases", "no stabs", "describe
sustained texture rather than an action genre" — I had diagnosed genre labels as the problem and
prescribed exactly the language that kills energy.

> **Franco's line: "Drums-free must not become motion-free." Ask for percussive melodic articulation.**

## THE DROP

**The drop lands on the exact first frame of the new world — bar 1, beat 1.**

**The beat gap belongs to the OUTGOING world.** Use the final 1/16 note, or at most the final 1/8, of
bar 8 as near-silence, tension, flash, suspended gesture or held breath. **Do not reveal the new
environment during that gap.** The clean new-world frame, the bass return, the hook attack and the
visual impact all arrive together on the downbeat.

**Correction to my proposal:** bars 1–6 should sit at **85–90% intensity, not permanent maximum**.
Without contrast, six drops become six ordinary downbeats.

---

## THE TWO-BAR BUILD

Two bars is enough *if every device points upward*.

**Bar 7** — start the riser, open or high-pass the outgoing overlay, increase melodic subdivision
(quarters/eighths → eighths/sixteenths), raise pitch gradually, reduce the low register.

**Bar 8** — shorten the notes, accelerate the roll from eighths to sixteenths (32nds only in the
final half-bar), widen the upper frequencies, push the pitch riser higher, then **remove everything
for the final 1/16 or 1/8**.

**Avoid:** long breakdowns, slow filter journeys, full chord-progression detours, tempo ramps,
four-bar vocal phrases, huge reverb washes, a full beat of silence.

> *"Two bars cannot support an opera house renovation."*

---

## WHERE THE BUILD LIVES — NOT IN THE SPINE

The spine stays **unchanged and metronomic**, per Nelson. Builds live in two other places:

| Layer | Carries |
|---|---|
| **World overlay** | filter automation, rising arpeggios, pitch lifts, harmonic tension, denser melodic figures |
| **Transition-effects stem** (new, separate) | snare rolls, noise risers, reverses, impacts, uplifters |

Splitting them is what lets the melodic overlays stay drums-free while the transitions still hit.

For the **audience mix**, briefly gate the whole music bus during that final 1/16–1/8 to create the
true beat gap. This does not alter the E1 source file. For **Higgsfield motion conditioning**, keep
E1 constant underneath unless testing proves the gap improves the transition.

**Binary divisions beat 3+2+2+1.** Two patterns only:

## QA NOTE

The band-split detector still applies — mid-band 300–2000 Hz onset rate separates drums from
melodic attacks with no overlap. But **it must not be used to reject energy.** These overlays are
*supposed* to be dense and percussive in articulation. The test is whether a **drum kit** is present,
not whether attacks are present. See `MUSIC_METHOD.md`.
