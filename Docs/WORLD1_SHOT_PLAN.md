Shot 1, push-off and introduction. 0:00–0:02.8125, 1.5 bars. Cut on bar 2, beat 3.

Shot size: low medium-full, emphasizing her legs, board and lower torso.

Lens: 35mm.

Camera height: 20–30 cm above the pavement, near skateboard-axle height.

Angle: three-quarter rear-left.

Camera movement: stabilized tracking move following Paola as she pushes away from camera, traveling diagonally from the foreground-left toward the midground-right. The camera maintains roughly the same distance and lets the neighborhood flow around her. Near the end, it drifts slightly outward toward her left side, revealing more of her profile without becoming a full lateral shot.

Action: one strong push, possibly a second shorter push, then both feet settle onto the board. Her body lowers naturally into speed.

Framing: begin with Paola large in the left half of frame. Keep her anchored around the left-center as she moves deeper into the street. Leave open road toward screen-right.

Last frame and handoff: both feet are planted, her forward momentum is established, and we can read a partial side profile. Her body is moving diagonally away but still clearly biased left-to-right. The camera has begun unwrapping toward the side position needed for Shot 2.

Shot 2, speed build and carve. 0:02.8125–0:07.5000, 2.5 bars. Cut on bar 5, beat 1.

Shot size: full shot moving toward a wider full shot.

Lens: 24–28mm, preferably 26mm if available.

Camera height: knee height, approximately 45–60 cm above pavement.

Angle: begins rear three-quarter-left and gradually becomes a clean left-side profile.

Camera movement: wrapped tracking move, not a full orbit. The camera accelerates slightly, eases outward from behind her and settles into a parallel lateral track. As it moves outward, the framing widens enough to reveal parked cars, palms, road texture and environmental speed.

Action: Paola performs two committed carves. The first introduces the lateral body movement. The second is deeper and more forceful, loading her weight for the upcoming trick.

Framing: keep her close to center-left, with the street opening toward screen-right. Background vehicles and road markings should move rapidly behind her, but her body and skateboard remain crisp and readable.

Last frame and handoff: she exits the second carve and begins straightening the board. Her knees are still flexed, shoulders square toward travel, and her weight begins dropping lower. This frame should already feel like she is preparing to crouch rather than merely continuing to cruise.

Shot 3, crouch and pop. 0:07.5000–0:09.3750, 1 bar. Cut on bar 6, beat 1.

Shot size: medium-full or tight full shot.

Lens: 35–40mm, preferably 40mm for stronger compression and focus on her mechanics.

Camera height: approximately 30–40 cm above pavement.

Angle: low three-quarter front-right, looking slightly back toward her as she approaches.

Camera movement: short reverse tracking move. The camera moves backward with her, holding her scale while she compresses. Add a subtle downward dip with her crouch, followed by the beginning of an upward tilt as she pops. No orbit.

Action: she drops into the crouch, shoulders and arms prepare, rear foot loads the tail, then she snaps the board upward. The actual takeoff begins at the end of the shot.

Framing: Paola is centered or slightly left of center. The board, both feet and her full body mechanics must remain visible. This is the first clear anticipation shot, the spring being compressed.

Last frame and handoff: the tail has struck, her body is beginning to rise and the board has just separated from the pavement. Her face may be partially readable, but the strongest face reveal is reserved for Shot 4. Cut during upward acceleration so the next shot inherits active lift rather than restarting the trick.

Shot 4, kickflip rotation and hero apex. 0:09.3750–0:15.0000, 3 bars. World transition lands on the next phrase’s beat 1.

Shot size: low-angle full-body hero shot.

Lens: 28–32mm, preferably 32mm.

Camera height: 15–25 cm above pavement.

Angle: three-quarter front-left, with Paola moving diagonally toward screen-right.

Camera movement: begin with a controlled low tracking move and upward tilt following her takeoff. As she reaches the upper half of the jump, reduce camera speed and ease into a floating hold. The shot should transition from normal-speed energy into controlled slow motion, not start slow immediately.

Action by bar:
Bar 6: takeoff and initial board rotation.
Bar 7: full kickflip rotation, knees rising and arms balancing.
Bar 8: rotation completes, board levels beneath her, and the motion stretches into the apex.

Framing: she rises from center-left toward a centered, elevated hero composition. At the apex, her face must be readable, her silhouette clean, and the skateboard fully separated from the background. Preserve negative space around her body.

Final frame and World 2 handoff: Paola is suspended at the apex, centered high in frame, knees tucked, arms balanced, face visible and board level beneath her. Her movement still reads left-to-right. This exact scale, camera angle, vertical position, body geometry and horizon relationship become the Nano Pro wardrobe-swap frame for Bollywood. The skateboard is removed and her wardrobe changes, but the airborne pose and composition remain locked. World 2 begins on the next downbeat with her descending into the Bollywood street.

Overall visual progression: behind her and grounded, then beside her and fast, then in front of her as she loads the trick, then beneath her as she becomes the hero image.
