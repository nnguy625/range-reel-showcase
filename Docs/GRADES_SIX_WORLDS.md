## 🔑 THE CAPTURE RULING — governs all six

> **"My decision: one constant capture DNA across all six. I would not change the glass family per world."**

**The capture spine, constant everywhere:**

```
35mm anamorphic · tungsten-balanced 500-speed stock family · warm gentle lens character ·
soft highlight rolloff · consistent fine grain · restrained warm horizontal flare with the blue reduced
```

His reasoning, and it is the strongest argument in the whole ruling:

> *"If you changed the glass per world, you would gain some instant genre separation, but you would lose
> something more important: **the body-rhyme cuts would weaken.** The viewer may not name it, but the shift
> in facial geometry, flare behavior, edge falloff and background rendering would make Paola feel like she
> is being **reinterpreted rather than continuously observed**."*

> *"Six distinct films in mood, while preserving **one photographic consciousness** across the whole reel."*

🔑 **This directly protects the six match cuts.** The seams only work if her body reads as the same body
across the cut — change the glass and the rhyme breaks at exactly the moment it has to land. Capture is
constant; **the genre shift lives entirely in colour and light.**

**Consequence for every prompt:** the capture block above is boilerplate in all six worlds. Only the
world's grade block below changes.

## W1 — **BLEACHED-GOLD SOCAL**

| Field | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | Chalky warm whites, faded peach and terracotta, dry straw, dusty olive, pale cyan-blue sky, natural warm skin. **Sun-softened rather than desaturated.** |
| **Key light** | Hard late-afternoon sun from **high three-quarter back or side**. Strong pavement and stucco bounce keeps her face readable without looking artificially filled. |
| **Contrast / blacks** | Medium-low. Highlights **broad and creamy**, shadows open. Blacks **softened charcoal — never milky or faded away**. |
| **Anchor** | **Bleached gold** |

> *"Warmth without nostalgia. Young, immediate and physically sunlit — not vintage for vintage's sake."*

## W2 — **DUST-TEAL RADIANCE**

| Field | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | Dusty teal architecture and wardrobe masses, turmeric gold, antique brass, muted earth, warm skin, and **one clean heroic red reserved for Paola**. |
| **Key light** | Broad luminous market daylight filtered through awnings and fabric, **primarily frontal three-quarter**. Thin shafts of harder sun may enter from **high rear angles and illuminate suspended dust**. |
| **Contrast / blacks** | Medium, bright glowing midtones. Shadows stay **colourful and readable**. Blacks warm-neutral, **slightly lifted — never dense enough to drain the celebration**. |
| **Anchor** | **Dust-lit jewel** |

> *"Glow from reflected colour, not global saturation. Theatrical abundance photographed with restraint."*

🔑 **The red is Paola's alone** — she is the only heroic red in a teal world, so she separates from the
crowd **by hue rather than by lighting.** That quietly solves the W2 crowd-separation problem.

## W5 — **SOLEMN MOONLIGHT** *(was a blank page — this is the new work)*

| Field | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | Moon-silver, indigo-charcoal, weathered cedar brown, muted pine green, rice-paper amber, steel grey, and **pale mineral sakura pink**. |
| **Key light** | A **large, soft but directional moon key from high side-back**, giving silver edges to hair, blade and garden forms — with a **restrained warm counter-source from lantern or shoji glow** lifting the face and kimono folds. |
| **Contrast / blacks** | Deep, solemn. Blacks sit in **velvety charcoal and hold detail** in wood, foliage and fabric. Highlights stay **silver-grey rather than bright white**. |
| **Anchor** | **Solemn moonlight** |

> *"Serious, historical and emotionally weighted — **not stylized samurai cool**, but dignity, danger and
> silence under night air."*

🔑 **"Pale mineral sakura pink" resolves the kimono.** The pink is canon, and he has folded it into the
palette as a *mineral* rather than a sweet pink — so it reads as period-correct dyed silk under moonlight,
not costume. That is the Last Samurai note answered precisely.
