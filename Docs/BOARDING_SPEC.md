## 🔒 THE FOUR GOVERNING RULINGS

### 1. Screen direction — one flip, deliberately placed

> *"Maintain Paola's dominant travel left-to-right across the reel unless a deliberate reversal marks
> a narrative turn. Flip only once, preferably entering the katana world, where opposition and combat
> justify right-to-left motion. Establish the reversal with a frontal or neutral-axis shot first,
> never by accidental 180-degree crossing."*

**L→R is the default for worlds 1–4. World 5 flips to R→L, and W5-S1 (the standoff) is the
frontal/neutral-axis shot that earns the flip.** World 6 returns to L→R for the closing walk.

⚠ Screen direction is a known blind spot on this project. **Never eyeball it** — draw the top-down
map, stand at camera, use the cross-product. "The reverse angle flips it" is false unless it is a
clean 180.

### 2. Which cuts need true end-frame → start-frame anchoring

| Cut | Franco's call | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 1 → 2 skate → Bollywood | **ANCHOR** | end-frame of W1-S4 IS the start-frame of W2-S1 |
| 2 → 3 Bollywood → gun-fu | independent | hidden by the fabric wipe |
| 3 → 4 gun-fu → car | **ANCHOR** | arms-extended → hands-on-wheel, pose match |
| 4 → 5 car → katana | independent | hidden by impact / hard downbeat |
| 5 → 6 katana → runway | independent | hidden by the particle dissolve |
| 6 → end runway → sidewalk | **ANCHOR** | continuous stride behind a full-frame occluder |

*"...can be independent if hidden by a whip, flash, impact, foreground wipe, or hard downbeat cut."*

⚠ **This differs from `BEAT_MAP.md` on two cuts and Franco's version is sharper.** The beat map
called 3→4 a hard cut and 4→5 a hard match. Franco swaps them, and the reason holds: **a POSE match
needs frame anchoring** because limb geometry has to line up exactly (3→4, arms → wheel); **a MOTION
match needs only direction and speed continuity** (4→5, rotation), so it can survive as two
independent generations. Adopt Franco's. Flagged for Nelson.

### 3. Match-cut priority order — what must match beyond the pose

1. Screen position and scale
2. Motion direction and speed
3. Camera height and perspective
4. Silhouette and limb geometry
5. Eyeline and head angle
6. Lighting direction
7. Dominant colour / value
8. Background lines
9. Focal depth
10. Motion blur

**Check them in this order.** Position and scale failing makes the rest irrelevant.

### 4. Animatic BEFORE stills — 48 keyframes, not 24

> *"Build an animatic first from 48 rough keyframes: one start and one required end frame per shot.
> Cut them against the locked 90-second spine, exact bar map, builds, gaps, and drops. Use crude
> grayscale proxies with lens, direction, framing, and endpoint notes. **Do not generate polished
> stills until the animatic proves every handoff.**"*

This changes the plan: **48 rough grayscale proxies → animatic against the real spine → only then
the polished stills.** The animatic is what proves the handoffs and the rhythm before anything
expensive is generated.
